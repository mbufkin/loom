# Curriculum feedback form research

**Ticket:** [09-curriculum-feedback-form-research](../tickets/09-curriculum-feedback-form-research.md)  
**Question:** Strong short written feedback patterns for reviewing lesson plans / written curriculum — especially **why** a rating was given and **what to improve** — how practitioners judge the artifact; what Loom should copy, adapt, or avoid vs Path A one-pager + hard-gate trust checklist.  
**Compared against:** [04-one-page-path-a-prototype](./04-one-page-path-a-prototype.md), [06-trust-scorecard-example](./06-trust-scorecard-example.md)  
**Date:** 2026-08-04  
**Scope:** Written curriculum / lesson-plan audit (CTE + daily plans). Not classroom walkthrough Domains 2–3 as primary.

---

## 1. Verdict (short)

The strongest short curriculum-feedback artifacts share one skeleton: **claim → evidence/why → feed-forward cue**, on a **priority-first** page, judged by whether a teacher can act in minutes without the reviewer rewriting the lesson. Loom’s draft one-pager + Core 4 hard-gates already match that skeleton; copy UbD’s feedback-vs-guidance split and EdReports-style “missing fails the gate,” adapt coach “evidence → meaning → next step” into materials language, avoid observation scores, essay narratives, and full peer-review rubrics as the shipped artifact.

---

## 2. Patterns that show up again and again

### Pattern A — Rating + rationale + next step (coach / observation tools, adapted to documents)

Common three-part writeup (TeachBoost-style coaching notes; Observation Partner “evidence → rubric → next step”; Trellis “strengths → growth → next steps”):

| Slot | Job | Length norm |
|------|-----|-------------|
| **Evidence / Why** | Low-inference what is (or isn’t) in the plan | 1–2 sentences |
| **Meaning / Rating** | Tie to a shared criterion (rubric cell, pass, design standard) | word or short label |
| **Next step / Improve** | One high-leverage action the author can do soon | 1 concrete cue |

**Why + improve handling:** Why is descriptive and goal-referenced (what the criterion asked for vs what the document shows). Improve is a **cue**, not a draft lesson — “add X / clarify Y,” not pasted objectives and activities.

**Sources:** [Observation Partner — actionable feedback](https://observationpartner.com/resources/core-observation-evidence/turning-observation-notes-into-actionable-teacher-feedback); [SchoolStatus / TeachBoost activity notes](https://www.schoolstatus.com/blog/product-updates-coach); [Trellis observation feedback guide](https://gettrellis.app/blog/how-to-write-teacher-observation-feedback-a-time-saving-guide-for-administrators).

### Pattern B — Feed up / feed back / feed forward (Hattie & Timperley)

Three questions every useful feedback answer should cover:

1. **Where are we going?** (criterion / success look)  
2. **How are we going?** (status vs that criterion — the rating + why)  
3. **Where to next?** (feed-forward — the improve)

**Why + improve handling:** “How am I going?” without “Where to next?” is incomplete; “more work” is a weak feed-forward. Strong improve specifies *what kind of change* closes the gap.

**Source:** [Hattie & Timperley, *The Power of Feedback*](https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487) (summary framing also in [Duke Kunshan CTL](https://ctl.dukekunshan.edu.cn/resources/teaching-guides/feedback-for-learning/)).

### Pattern C — Wiggins: feedback ≠ advice ≠ evaluation

Wiggins’ seven keys (goal-referenced, tangible, actionable, user-friendly, timely, ongoing, consistent) plus the hard distinction:

- **Feedback** = information about performance vs a goal (descriptive)  
- **Advice / guidance** = what to try next  
- **Evaluation** = judgment / grade  

**Why + improve handling:** Best short forms *label both*: Why ≈ feedback (descriptive gap); Improve ≈ guidance (how to close it). Vague praise/blame (“good job,” “weak plan”) fails the actionable test.

**Sources:** [ASCD — Seven Keys to Effective Feedback](https://www.ascd.org/el/articles/seven-keys-to-effective-feedback); [Authentic Education — Feedback what it is and isn’t](https://authenticeducation.org/feedback-what-it-is-and-isnt/).

### Pattern D — UbD peer review: Feedback vs Guidance on design standards

UbD peer review process (individual silent review → group form → conversation with designer):

- Comments tied to **design standards**, not taste  
- **Feedback** = intent vs effect (does the design meet the stated goals?)  
- **Guidance** = how to narrow the gap — without substituting the reviewer’s preferred unit  
- Typical quality bands on design rubrics: e.g. exemplary / satisfactory / needs improvement (three levels)

**Why + improve handling:** Explicit split matches Loom’s Why + Improve. Guidance should improve *their* design against shared standards — not rewrite the lesson for them.

**Sources:** [ASCD UbD Guide HighQU downloads (design standards + feedback/guidance)](https://files.ascd.org/pdfs/publications/books/UBD_Guide_HighQU_downloads.pdf); [McTighe UbD white paper](https://jaymctighe.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/UbD-White-Paper.pdf); [LDC & UbD complementary frameworks (Design Standards excerpt)](https://ldc.org/sites/default/files/research/JMcTighe_LDC%20and%20UbD%20Complementary%20Frameworks.pdf); example peer forms such as [Cortland EDU 331 peer review](https://www2.cortland.edu/offices/ict/files-to-share/Episode%201%20Resource%20Peer%20Review%20Form.pdf) (comments required when below Meets).

### Pattern E — Danielson Domain 1 as *document* criteria (not Domains 2–3 scores)

Domain 1 (Planning & Preparation) supplies criteria for written plans: outcomes clarity, coherent sequence, assessment design, knowledge of students/resources — usable as a **review lens on materials**, not as an observation score dump.

**Why + improve handling:** Strong Domain-1 document review cites what the plan shows about outcomes/assessment/coherence; weak practice pastes 1a–1f ratings as if a walkthrough happened.

**Sources:** [Danielson Group 2022 FFT draft](https://danielsongroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/2022-Framework-for-Teaching_Draft_June-28-2022-.pdf); [OSPI 2022 Framework guide](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2023-08/2022-framework-teaching.pdf).

### Pattern F — EdReports gateways (hard-gate thinking for materials)

EdReports reviews materials in **gateways**: fail early gates → not eligible for later depth. Evidence guides force indicator → evidence → score. Ratings are about **materials quality/usability**, not teacher performance.

**Why + improve handling:** Short consumer-facing summaries emphasize whether gateways were met and where evidence failed — not essays. Gateway logic = “MISSING / fail non-negotiable stops the fancy scoring.”

**Sources:** [EdReports Review Tools](https://edreports.org/process/review-tools); [EdReports FAQs (gateways / NE)](https://edreports.org/about/faqs); example criteria PDF [ELA 3–8 v1.5 Review Criteria](https://cdn.edreports.org/reviewtools/ela/1.5/ELA-Grades-3-8-v1.5-Tools_Review-Criteria.pdf).

### Pattern G — Instructional rounds writeups (description → patterns → next level of work)

Harvard-style rounds: low-inference description, pattern talk, **next level of work** — focused on the instructional core / problem of practice, **not** evaluating an individual teacher. Writeups stay descriptive; judgment is collective and systemic.

**Why + improve handling:** “Next level of work” ≈ improve at system level. For a *single lesson-plan audit one-pager*, borrow the **descriptive evidence + next work** habit; do **not** borrow classroom-observation logistics as the product.

**Sources:** [HGSE — Treating the instructional core](https://www.gse.harvard.edu/ideas/usable-knowledge/09/05/treating-instructional-core-education-rounds); [Principal’s Playbook — rounds protocol](https://www.theprincipalsplaybook.com/instructional-leadership/instructional-rounds-professional-learning-peer-feedback-protocol).

### Pattern H — Curriculum / lesson-plan audit one-pagers

Practical coach audit sheets tend to:

- Checklist or 3-level scale on a **single page** (e.g. Meets / Approaches / Not Observed)  
- Force comments on fails or near-fails  
- Lead with gaps / alignment / next actions for a planning meeting  

**Why + improve handling:** Binary “present?” alone is weak; forms that require a comment when Developing/Incomplete encode Why. Action-step sections encode Improve.

**Sources:** Peer-review / lesson-plan forms that require comments below Meets (e.g. [Cortland form](https://www2.cortland.edu/offices/ict/files-to-share/Episode%201%20Resource%20Peer%20Review%20Form.pdf)); one-page rigor-audit style checklists in coaching practice (structure pattern; treat commercial checklists as illustrations of density, not authority).

### Pattern I — Knight partnership coaching (tone / ownership)

Knight: prefer collaborative exploration of data over top-down “do this my way.” Opinions offered provisionally; teacher agency preserved.

**Why + improve handling:** Written improve should read as **cues the author owns**, not a scripted rewrite. Fits Loom’s “auditor cues, never draft lesson text.”

**Sources:** [Idaho TC — What Good Coaches Do (Knight)](https://idahotc.com/Portals/0/Resources/402/What%20Good%20Coaches%20Do.pdf); [Marzano Evaluation Center coaching/feedback summary](https://marzanoevaluationcenter.com/library/instructional-coaching/).

## 3. How practitioners judge whether the feedback *artifact* is good

Across coaching, UbD peer review, and materials review, the artifact itself is judged roughly like this:

| Test | Pass looks like | Fail looks like |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Actionable** (Wiggins) | Reader knows what to add/clarify next | “Needs work,” letter grade only |
| **Goal-referenced** | Tied to named criteria (pass, design standard, gateway) | Personal taste / “I would have taught it differently” |
| **Evidence-backed Why** | Short pointer to what’s in/out of the document | Judgment with no document anchor |
| **Feed-forward present** | Explicit next cue | Status-only checklist |
| **User-friendly length** | Skimmable in a few minutes; few priorities | Multi-page essay or 40-item dump |
| **Guidance ≠ rewrite** (UbD) | Cues close the gap in *their* design | Reviewer pastes a replacement lesson |
| **Right object of judgment** | Materials / plan usability | Observation / T-TESS delivery scores on a plan review |
| **Trust / consistency** | Same criteria, same shape every time | Soft “vibes” page that changes by reviewer |
| **Priority** | Highest-leverage gaps first | Equal weight on every checkbox |

This maps cleanly onto Loom’s **Core 4 hard-gates** (gaps have why+improve; ~2 min / one page for the *feedback* artifact; improve doesn’t write the lesson; no essays / observation scores) — those gates are a practitioner-grade trust test of the *page*, not of the teacher.

## 4. Concrete form shapes (what to steal structurally)

### Shape 1 — Priority gaps stack (best for one-pagers)

```
## Top gaps
1. [Criterion] — [MISSING | PRESENT · Weak]
   - Why: …
   - Improve: …   ← cue, not draft text

## What’s working (short)
[table or 3 bullets: criterion · quality · why]

## Glance / count (optional)
PRESENT n/N · focus: …

## Evidence pointers
- short cites only
```

**Matches Loom prototype section order.** Aligns with coach “growth first when time is scarce,” EdReports “fail the gate first,” and rounds “next level of work.”

### Shape 2 — UbD / peer matrix (good for deep design review; heavy for ship)

Rows = design standards; columns = rating + feedback + guidance. Powerful in PLC peer review; **too dense** as the default Path A consumer page if every A2–A7 row is fully expanded.

### Shape 3 — Evidence → meaning → next step cards (good for coach tools)

One card per focus area; max 1–2 cards per visit. For curriculum audit, convert “observed” to “found in plan.”

### Shape 4 — Gateway strip (good for trust / eligibility)

```
Gate: language supports | PASS/FAIL
Gate: access notes     | PASS/FAIL
… (non-negotiables)
Quality labels only after PRESENT / gate open
```

**Matches Loom X6** (Strong/Adequate/Weak only on PRESENT) and EdReports NE logic.

## 5. Compare to Loom draft

**Local drafts:** [04-one-page-path-a-prototype](./04-one-page-path-a-prototype.md), [06-trust-scorecard-example](./06-trust-scorecard-example.md). Locked context: A1–A8 kept; why+improve on A2–A7; improve = auditor cues; quality labels only on PRESENT; order gaps → working → Hunter → evidence; Core 4 hard-gates.

### Keep (already world-class aligned)

| Loom choice | Why research backs it |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **Why + Improve on every top gap** | Hattie feed-back + feed-forward; Wiggins actionable; UbD feedback + guidance |
| **Improve = cue, never drafted lesson** | UbD “don’t substitute your design”; Knight partnership; Core 4 C3 |
| **Gaps first, then what’s working** | Priority coaching; audit one-pagers; rounds “next level of work” |
| **Strong / Adequate / Weak only on PRESENT** | EdReports-style: don’t quality-score what’s not there; Core X6 |
| **~1 page / ~2 min for the feedback artifact** | Wiggins user-friendly; coach writeup length norms |
| **Short evidence pointers, not table dumps** | Low-inference evidence habit; Core X7 / C4 |
| **Hard-gate trust scorecard on the page** | EdReports gateways; consistent judgment of the artifact |
| **No observation / T-TESS teaching scores** | Domain 1 document lens ≠ Domains 2–3 walkthrough |

### Adapt (small upgrades from the field)

| Idea | Adaptation for Loom |
|------|---------------------|
| **UbD Feedback vs Guidance labels** | Optionally rename or subtitle Why → “Feedback (what’s in/out)” and Improve → “Guidance (cue)” in docs — same fields, clearer theory |
| **Cap top gaps at 2–3** | Coach tools stress 1–2 high-leverage growth areas; prototype’s 3 is at the upper edge — keep 3 max for CTE audits |
| **One “focus improve” line in Hunter glance** | Already present; treat as the rounds-style “next level of work” strip — protect it in copy |
| **Comments-required-on-fail** | Peer forms require comments when below Meets — encode as hard rule: no MISSING/Weak without Why+Improve (C1) |
| **Domain 1 language for outcomes/assessment** | When writing Why for A2/A3/A4, prefer “observable finish line / aligned check” phrasing over generic “unclear” |
| **Core 4 + X5–X7 for ship tests** | Core 4 alone matches Wiggins/actionability; X5–X7 lock the shape already agreed — use both for golden fixtures |

### Avoid (relative to draft — do not regress)

| Temptation | Why avoid |
|------------|-----------|
| **Full UbD 15-standard peer matrix as the default page** | Excellent for PLC deep review; kills 2-minute read |
| **Observation Domains 2–3 / T-TESS delivery scores** | Wrong object; violates C4 |
| **Essay narrative or “glow and grow” paragraphs without structure** | Fails length + actionability |
| **Improve that pastes rewritten objectives/activities** | UbD anti-pattern; fails C3 |
| **Quality labels on MISSING** | Breaks gateway logic / X6 |
| **Equal-weight 15-item rigor walkthrough of the *plan*** | Observation-audit hybrid; crowds out top gaps |
| **Top-down scripted “teach it this way”** | Knight partnership conflict; ownership drop |
| **Grading the teacher instead of the materials page** | Trust scorecard is for *our* artifact (06), not HR |

## 6. Implications for Path A copy (practical)

1. **Ship the skeleton, not the PLC binder** — Pattern A/C/D fields inside Shape 1.  
2. **Treat Core 4 as non-negotiable gateways** — any missing Why/Improve or lesson rewrite = FAIL the feedback artifact.  
3. **Keep quality bands three-valued and PRESENT-only** — same job as UbD exemplary/satisfactory/needs-improvement, scoped to usable teaching path.  
4. **Evidence pointers stay short** — enough to locate, not enough to re-read the unit.  
5. **CTE audit voice** — Why talks about what’s in the *written* plan (ELPS, accommodations, objective finish line, formative path); Improve tells the author what kind of note/scaffold/clarity to add.

---

## 7. Gist for the map

- Strong short curriculum feedback = **claim → why (evidence vs criterion) → improve (feed-forward cue)** on a **priority-first** page.  
- Practitioners judge the *artifact* by actionability, goal-reference, evidence, feed-forward, length, and “guidance ≠ rewrite” — Loom’s **Core 4 hard-gates** already encode that.  
- **Copy:** UbD feedback/guidance split; Hattie/Wiggins why+next; EdReports “missing fails the gate”; coach 1–3 priority gaps.  
- **Adapt:** Cap gaps at ≤3; optional Feedback/Guidance labels; Domain-1 wording for outcomes/assessment Whys; test with Core 4 + X5–X7.  
- **Avoid:** Observation scores, essay narratives, full UbD matrices as the default ship page, quality labels on MISSING, improve that drafts the lesson.  
- Loom prototype order (**gaps → working → Hunter → evidence**) and PRESENT-only Strong/Adequate/Weak are aligned with field practice — keep.  
- Improve remains **auditor cues** the author owns (Knight/UbD), never substitute curriculum text.
