# Curriculum feedback form research

## Question

What are strong **short written feedback** patterns for reviewing lesson plans / written curriculum (coach tools, instructional rounds writeups, curriculum audit one-pagers, UbD/peer review forms) — especially how they handle **why** a rating was given and **what to improve** — and how do practitioners judge whether that feedback artifact is good? What should Loom copy, adapt, or avoid relative to our draft one-pager + hard-gate trust checklist?

## Answer

Full writeup: [09-curriculum-feedback-form-research](../assets/09-curriculum-feedback-form-research.md).

**Gist:** Strong short curriculum feedback = **claim → why (evidence vs criterion) → improve (feed-forward cue)** on a priority-first page. Practitioners judge the artifact by actionability, goal-reference, evidence, feed-forward, length, and “guidance ≠ rewrite.” Loom’s one-pager + Core 4 already match; **copy** UbD feedback/guidance split, Hattie/Wiggins why+next, EdReports-style missing fails the gate, coach 1–3 priority gaps; **adapt** cap gaps ≤3 and test with Core 4 + X5–X7; **avoid** observation scores, essays, dense matrices as the ship page, quality labels on MISSING, improve that drafts the lesson.
