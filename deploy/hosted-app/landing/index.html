How it works

From a folder of documents to a unit-by-unit review

1

Read

Add your lesson plans, slides, handouts, quizzes and rubrics. Loom reads each one in full — not a summary or a clipped excerpt.

2

Organise

Loom works out what each document is and which unit it belongs to, so a pile of files becomes a structured course.

3

Review

Each unit is checked against what a complete unit needs. The report separates what is present, what is missing, and what only you can decide.

How Loom behaves

Built to audit your curriculum, not to rewrite it

It reads the whole document

Long documents are read in parts and combined, never cut down to save time. If it cannot read a file, it tells you.

It never writes your curriculum

Loom reports on what exists. It does not generate lessons, assessments or rubrics, or change your files.

You can check every finding

Findings point back to the document and the text they came from, so a reviewer can verify instead of trusting.

Unclear cases go to you

When the documents are ambiguous — duplicates, conflicting versions — Loom flags a decision rather than guessing.
