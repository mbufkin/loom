Unit 2 — Oxy-Fuel Cutting and Compressed Gas Safety

Course: Introduction to Welding Technology (CTE 1042)
Unit length: 2 instructional days, 50-minute periods
Setting: Welding lab, cutting bay
Placement: Weeks 3–4, immediately following Unit 1

Unit objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

  1. Assemble an oxy-acetylene outfit in the correct order and leak-test it with soap solution.
  2. Set regulator working pressures appropriate to the tip size in use.
  3. Light, adjust to a neutral flame, and shut down a cutting torch using the correct sequence.
  4. Perform a straight freehand cut on 3/8" mild steel plate with a kerf within 1/8" of the scribed line.
  5. State the required separation distance for oxygen and fuel gas cylinder storage, and explain why it is required.

Standards alignment

Aligned to state CTE Manufacturing cluster standards MFG.WLD.3 (thermal cutting processes) and MFG.SAF.2 (compressed gas handling and storage).

Day 1 — Cylinders, assembly and leak testing

Objective for the day: Students assemble and leak-test an outfit, and pass the written safety assessment. No torch is lit on Day 1.

Opening (10 minutes)

Show an acetylene cylinder and an oxygen cylinder side by side. Ask what happens if the oxygen cylinder is knocked over and the valve shears off. Establish the number: a full oxygen cylinder holds around 2,200 psi, enough to drive the cylinder through a concrete block wall. This is the reason cylinders are chained upright with the cap on whenever a regulator is not fitted — not departmental fussiness.

Direct instruction (20 minutes)

Cylinder identification and handling:

  • Oxygen: green in this shop, right-hand threads, valve opened fully against the back seat.
  • Acetylene: red in this shop, left-hand threads with a notched nut, valve opened no more than three quarters of a turn so it can be shut quickly.
  • Acetylene is unstable above 15 psi and must never be used above that working pressure. It is stored dissolved in acetone within a porous filler, which is also why a cylinder must stand upright for at least 30 minutes before use if it has been lying down.
  • Storage: oxygen and fuel gas cylinders separated by 20 feet, or by a five-foot non-combustible barrier with a half-hour fire rating.

Assembly order, demonstrated then repeated by students: crack each cylinder valve briefly to clear the seat, fit the correct regulator to the correct cylinder, attach hoses (green to oxygen, red to fuel), fit the torch handle, fit the cutting attachment and tip, then open cylinder valves slowly with the regulators backed out.

Guided practice (15 minutes)

In pairs, students assemble an outfit at the bench and leak-test every joint with soap solution while the instructor checks each one. Any bubble means the joint comes apart and is refitted. Nothing is lit this period.

Safety assessment and closing (5 minutes)

Written safety assessment. As in Unit 1, a pass at 100% is required before torch time, and missed items are re-taught and re-assessed the same period.

Day 2 — Lighting, flame adjustment and the graded cut

Objective for the day: Students light and adjust a torch and perform a graded straight cut.

Safety verification (5 minutes)

Confirm every student has passed the written safety assessment. Students who have not do not receive a torch; they repeat the assembly and leak-test exercise at the bench.

Demonstration (15 minutes)

Instructor sets 40 psi oxygen and 7 psi acetylene for a size 0 tip, lights with a striker held to the side of the tip, and adjusts through the three flame types, naming each while it is visible:

  • Carburising: excess acetylene, feathered secondary cone, adds carbon to the steel.
  • Neutral: sharply defined inner cone, equal balance — the flame used for cutting.
  • Oxidising: excess oxygen, short pointed cone with a harsh hiss, burns the steel and widens the kerf.

Then demonstrate the cut: preheat to a bright cherry red at the plate edge, press the cutting oxygen lever, and travel at a speed that keeps the sparks blowing through and down rather than back up at the operator.

Booth practice and graded cut (25 minutes)

Students make at least three practice cuts on 3/8" plate, then one graded cut along a scribed line. Scored on kerf within 1/8" of the line, squareness of the cut face, and the amount of adhering slag on the underside.

Closing and shutdown (5 minutes)

Shutdown sequence, performed by every student and checked: cutting oxygen lever released, torch oxygen valve closed, fuel valve closed, both cylinder valves closed, then hoses bled by reopening the torch valves briefly, and both regulators backed out to zero.

Differentiation

Students who reach criteria early move to cutting a 2-inch circle from a template, which requires continuous torch-angle correction. Students needing more time cut on 1/4" plate first, where a slower travel speed is more forgiving, before returning to 3/8".

Materials for this unit

  • 3/8" mild steel plate, 6" x 6" coupons, 4 per student
  • 1/4" mild steel plate for differentiated practice
  • Oxy-acetylene outfits, one per two students, with size 0 cutting tips
  • Oxygen and acetylene cylinders under current hydrostatic test
  • Strikers with spare flints; no lighters or matches
  • Soapstone and soap solution for leak testing
  • Cutting goggles, shade 5, worn in addition to safety glasses
  • Leather gloves, flame-resistant jacket, leather boots

Instructor note

The cutting bay extraction fan must be running before any torch is lit, and the bay is swept clear of combustibles at the start of each period. The nearest fire extinguisher is checked at the beginning of the unit and its location pointed out to the class rather than assumed known.
