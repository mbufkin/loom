UNIT 1 - SHIELDED METAL ARC WELDING: VOCABULARY HANDOUT
Introduction to Welding Technology (CTE 1042)

Keep this handout in your shop folder. You will need the electrode table
during booth practice on Days 2 and 3.


TERMS

Arc
    The sustained electrical discharge between the electrode and the
    workpiece. Temperature at the arc reaches roughly 6,500 degrees F, which
    is hotter than the surface of molten steel by a wide margin and is why
    the pool forms almost instantly.

Arc length
    The distance between the tip of the electrode and the surface of the
    weld pool. Held at roughly the diameter of the electrode. Too long and
    the gas shield disperses, causing porosity; too short and the electrode
    freezes to the plate.

Arc flash (arc eye)
    A burn to the cornea caused by ultraviolet radiation from the arc.
    Symptoms usually appear six to twelve hours after exposure, so it is
    typically felt at home in the evening rather than in the shop. Prevented
    entirely by lowering the hood before striking an arc.

Base metal
    The material being welded, as distinct from the filler metal deposited
    by the electrode.

Bead
    The deposited weld metal left by a single pass of the electrode.

DCEP (direct current electrode positive)
    Polarity with the electrode connected to the positive terminal. Places
    roughly two thirds of the heat in the electrode, giving deeper
    penetration. Also called reverse polarity.

DCEN (direct current electrode negative)
    Polarity with the electrode connected to the negative terminal. Places
    more heat in the base metal, giving shallower penetration and a faster
    deposition rate. Also called straight polarity.

Electrode
    The consumable metal rod, coated in flux, that carries the current and
    supplies filler metal to the weld.

Electrode holder
    The insulated clamp that grips the electrode and connects it to the
    electrode lead. Hung clear of the welding table when not in use, never
    laid on it.

Flux
    The coating on the electrode. As it burns it produces a shielding gas
    that excludes atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen from the molten pool, and
    leaves a slag layer that slows cooling.

Fusion
    The melting together of base metal and filler metal into one continuous
    mass. Poor fusion may be invisible on the surface, which is why bead
    appearance alone does not prove a sound weld.

Kerf
    The width of material removed by a cutting process. Relevant in Unit 2
    rather than here, but listed because students meet the word in the shop.

Penetration
    The depth to which the weld extends into the base metal from its
    surface.

Polarity
    The direction of current flow in a DC welding circuit. See DCEP and
    DCEN. Reversed polarity is the most common setup error and produces a
    bead that can look acceptable while having poor fusion.

Porosity
    Gas pockets trapped in the solidified weld. Caused by a lost gas shield
    (long arc, wind, damp electrode) or contaminated base metal.

PPE (personal protective equipment)
    Helmet at shade 10 minimum, safety glasses under the helmet, leather
    gauntlet gloves, flame-resistant jacket or sleeves, leather boots.

Slag
    The solidified flux residue sitting on top of a finished bead. Chipped
    and brushed away before the next pass. Slag left between passes becomes
    a slag inclusion, which is a defect.

Spatter
    Droplets of molten metal thrown clear of the weld pool. Excessive
    spatter usually means amperage is too high or arc length too long.

Stringer bead
    A bead run in a straight line with little or no side-to-side motion.
    The first bead type taught, because it isolates travel speed and arc
    length from weaving technique.

Undercut
    A groove melted into the base metal along the toe of the weld and left
    unfilled. Reduces the effective thickness of the joint and is a rejectable
    defect under visual inspection.

Work clamp
    The clamp completing the welding circuit, attached to the workpiece or
    the table it rests on. Never attached through a chain, vice screw or
    bearing.

Work lead
    The cable running from the work clamp back to the machine. Carries the
    full welding current, like every other part of the circuit.


ELECTRODE SELECTION TABLE

  Electrode   Current      Use it for                  Watch out for
  ---------   ----------   -------------------------   --------------------
  E6010       DCEP only    Deep penetration on         Will not run on AC
                           dirty or painted steel

  E6011       AC or DC     Same digging arc as         Heavy spatter
                           E6010, tolerates AC

  E6013       AC or DC     Thin sheet, smooth          Shallow penetration
                           appearance, easiest

  E7018       DC or AC     Structural strength,        Must stay dry
                           clean ductile welds


READING AN ELECTRODE NUMBER: E7018

  E     electrode for arc welding
  70    70,000 psi minimum tensile strength
  1     all positions (2 would mean flat and horizontal only)
  8     low-hydrogen iron powder coating


AMPERAGE STARTING POINT

  Roughly 1 amp per 0.001 inch of electrode diameter.
  1/8 inch electrode = 0.125 inch = start near 125 amps.
  For 1/4 inch plate with 1/8 inch E7018: 110 to 130 amps DCEP.

  Then read the bead, not the dial:

  Too low    electrode sticks, arc dies, bead high and narrow
  Too high   heavy spatter, electrode burns back, undercut at the toes
  Correct    steady crackle, slag lifts in one piece, bead 1.5 to 2x
             electrode diameter wide
