Unit 1 — SMAW Fundamentals: Key Concepts

Student reading for Unit 1. Read before Day 2. You will need the electrode table during booth practice, so keep this handout in your shop folder.

What shielded metal arc welding is

Shielded metal arc welding joins metal by striking an electric arc between a consumable electrode and the workpiece. The arc melts both the base metal and the electrode, and the two mix into a single pool that solidifies as the weld. The electrode's flux coating burns as it goes, producing a gas shield that keeps oxygen and nitrogen out of the molten pool, plus a layer of slag that sits on top of the cooling bead and slows its cooling rate.

The name says what matters: the weld is shielded. Without that gas shield, atmospheric oxygen and nitrogen dissolve into the molten steel and come back out as porosity as it cools. This is also why welding in wind is a problem outdoors — the shield blows away, and the weld looks acceptable while being full of holes.

The parts of the circuit

Current leaves the machine, travels down the electrode lead to the electrode holder, crosses the arc gap into the workpiece, and returns through the work clamp and work lead. Every part of that loop carries the full welding current. Two consequences follow, and both are practical rather than theoretical:

  • A poor work clamp connection means a poor arc. If the arc is unstable and the machine is set correctly, check the clamp before changing anything else.
  • Current returns through whatever path is available. Clamp to the workpiece or the table it rests on — never through a chain, a vice screw or a bearing, all of which will arc internally and be destroyed.

Reading an electrode number

An AWS electrode designation packs four pieces of information into a short code. Reading E7018:

PartMeans
EElectrode for arc welding
7070,000 psi minimum tensile strength of the deposited weld
1Suitable for all positions: flat, horizontal, vertical, overhead
8Low-hydrogen iron powder coating; DC or AC

A third digit of 1 means all positions; 2 means flat and horizontal only. The fourth digit describes the coating and the current it wants.

E7018DC or AC Structural strength; clean, ductile welds on prepared steel Must stay dry — absorbed moisture causes hydrogen cracking

E7018 is kept in a heated rod oven at 250°F for this reason. An electrode that has sat out overnight in a humid shop goes back in the oven, not into a holder.

Setting amperage

Start from the manufacturer's range printed on the electrode box, then adjust by what you see. A rough starting point for mild steel is roughly 1 amp per 0.001" of electrode diameter, so 1/8" (0.125") electrode starts near 125 amps. For 1/4" plate with 1/8" E7018, 110 to 130 amps DCEP is a working window.

Read the bead rather than the dial:

  • Too low: the electrode sticks, the arc keeps going out, the bead sits high and narrow with poor fusion at the edges.
  • Too high: excessive spatter, the electrode burns back quickly, the bead is wide and flat, and undercut appears along the toes.
  • About right: a steady crackle, slag that lifts cleanly in one piece, and a bead one and a half to twice the electrode diameter in width.

Arc length

Hold an arc length roughly equal to the electrode diameter. Too long, and the gas shield spreads out and stops protecting the pool — you get porosity and a hollow, fluttering sound. Too short, and the electrode freezes to the plate. Arc length is the single habit that most separates a clean bead from a poor one, and it is the reason Day 2 is spent on practice beads rather than on a joint.

Personal protective equipment

Arc welding produces ultraviolet radiation intense enough to burn exposed skin and cornea in seconds. Required at all times in a booth:

  • Welding helmet, shade 10 minimum for currents up to 150 amps
  • Safety glasses worn under the helmet, for chipping slag with the helmet raised
  • Leather gauntlet gloves
  • Flame-resistant jacket or sleeves; no synthetic fabric, which melts
  • Leather boots; no canvas shoes, which spatter burns straight through

"Arc flash" or "arc eye" is a corneal burn that typically appears six to twelve hours after exposure, which is why it is usually felt at home that night rather than in the shop. It is painful, and it is entirely preventable by lowering the hood before striking an arc.
