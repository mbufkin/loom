Unit 1 — SMAW Fundamentals: Assessment Answer Key

Instructor copy. 10 items, 1 point each. Pass mark 7 of 10, with items 8–10 pass/fail independently as safety gate items.

1. Electrode — specifically, an electrode for arc welding. Accept "electrode". (1 point)

2. The minimum tensile strength of the deposited weld metal is 70,000 psi. Accept "70,000 psi tensile strength" or "70 ksi". Do not accept answers referring to amperage. (1 point)

3. b — the electrode may be used in all positions. (1 point)

4. E6011. It runs on alternating current, where E6010 requires DCEP, and its digging arc penetrates mill scale. Award the point for naming E6011 with either reason stated. A student who names E6011 but justifies it only by penetration has the right electrode for an incomplete reason — award the point and correct verbally, since the AC constraint is the one that actually rules E6010 out here. (1 point)

5. Its low-hydrogen coating absorbs moisture from the air. Moisture introduces hydrogen into the weld, which causes hydrogen cracking (also accepted: underbead cracking, embrittlement). The oven keeps the coating dry. Award the point for the moisture-to-cracking connection; "so it stays dry" alone is half an answer — prompt for what the moisture does. (1 point)

6. The amperage is set too low. They should increase it, working within the range printed on the electrode box — for 1/8" E7018 on 1/4" plate, toward 110–130 amps DCEP. Both the diagnosis and the adjustment are needed for the point. A common wrong answer is "arc too long", which produces the opposite signs: a fluttering arc and a wide, porous bead rather than sticking. (1 point)

7. b — about the same as the electrode diameter. (1 point)

8. Safety item. Shade 10 minimum. Accept shade 10 or any higher shade number. Do not accept a range beginning below 10. (1 point, pass/fail)

9. Safety item. Any two of the following, each with a correct hazard:

  • Safety glasses worn under the helmet — flying slag when chipping with the hood raised
  • Leather gauntlet gloves — burns from spatter, radiant heat and hot metal
  • Flame-resistant jacket or sleeves — spatter burns and UV exposure to the arms; synthetics melt onto skin
  • Leather boots — spatter burns through canvas footwear

Both items need a hazard attached. Naming two items with no hazards is half the item — prompt for what each protects against. (1 point, pass/fail)

10. Safety item. The work clamp attaches directly to the workpiece, or to the steel welding table the workpiece rests on. It must not be attached through a chain, a vice screw or a bearing, because the full welding current returns through the clamp: current will arc across the links or threads, damaging them and creating a fire and shock hazard away from where the student is looking. Award the point for correct placement plus the idea that current returns through the clamp and will arc through an indirect path. (1 point, pass/fail)

Re-teach notes

If more than a third of the class misses item 6, re-teach amperage symptoms before Day 3 using a deliberate demonstration: run a bead 30 amps low, then 30 amps high, then in range, and have students name each from the sound before seeing the bead. Sticking and porosity get confused because both are described as "the arc keeps dying", and the two have opposite corrections.

Item 10 is missed most often by students who answered from Day 1 notes rather than booth experience. The clamp point lands better after they have seen an arc mark on a vice screw than before.
