Unit 1 — Shielded Metal Arc Welding (SMAW) Fundamentals

Course: Introduction to Welding Technology (CTE 1042)
Unit length: 3 instructional days, 50-minute periods
Setting: Classroom for Day 1, welding lab for Days 2 and 3

Unit objectives

By the end of this unit, students will be able to:

  1. Identify the components of an SMAW machine and state the function of each.
  2. Select an appropriate electrode for a given base metal thickness, joint and welding position.
  3. Set polarity and amperage within the electrode manufacturer's published range.
  4. Run a flat-position stringer bead on 1/4" mild steel with consistent bead width and no visible undercut.
  5. Demonstrate correct personal protective equipment for arc welding, including selection of the appropriate filter shade.

Standards alignment

Aligned to state CTE Manufacturing cluster standards MFG.WLD.2 (welding processes and equipment) and MFG.SAF.1 (shop and personal safety). Weld quality criteria follow AWS D1.1 visual inspection acceptance for fillet welds, adapted for an introductory course.

Day 1 — Equipment, electrodes and safety

Objective for the day: Students identify SMAW equipment and explain electrode classification before any arc time.

Opening (10 minutes)

Display a cut-away photograph of a completed stringer bead alongside one with heavy undercut. Ask students which they would trust in a trailer hitch and why. Collect answers without correcting them; return to this at the end of Day 3.

Direct instruction (20 minutes)

Walk the machine: primary disconnect, polarity switch, amperage control, electrode holder, work clamp, ground path. Emphasise that the work clamp must attach to the workpiece or the table it sits on, never through a chain or a vice screw, because current will find another path and arc where it is not expected.

Introduce electrode classification using the AWS system. Work through E7018 digit by digit: 70 for 70,000 psi tensile strength, 1 for all positions, 8 for a low-hydrogen iron-powder coating requiring dry storage. Contrast with E6011, which runs on AC, digs through mill scale and light rust, and tolerates the conditions a beginner actually welds in.

Guided practice (15 minutes)

In pairs, students complete the equipment identification diagram and the electrode selection table on the vocabulary handout. Circulate and check the polarity column specifically — reversed polarity is the most common error and produces a bead that looks acceptable but has poor fusion.

Closing (5 minutes)

Exit question, verbal: "Your plate is 1/4" mild steel with mill scale, and the machine is AC only. Which electrode, and why?" Expected answer: E6011, because it operates on AC and its digging arc penetrates mill scale.

Day 2 — Machine setup and first beads

Objective for the day: Students set up a machine to specification and run practice beads on plate.

Safety verification (10 minutes)

Before any booth assignment, each student passes the written safety assessment at 100%. Any missed item is re-taught immediately and re-assessed. Students who have not passed work the equipment identification review at the classroom table; they do not enter a booth.

Demonstration (10 minutes)

Instructor demonstrates striking the arc with both the scratch and tap methods, then runs a 6-inch stringer bead at 110 amps DCEP with 1/8" E7018. Narrate arc length aloud while welding — roughly the electrode diameter — and show deliberately what a long arc sounds and looks like, then correct it.

Booth practice (25 minutes)

Students run a minimum of six practice beads on 1/4" plate. Target: 1/4" to 3/8" bead width, straight travel, complete slag release. Instructor circulates with a chipping hammer and inspects each bead in place before the next.

Closing (5 minutes)

Booth shutdown sequence: machine off at the disconnect, electrode removed from the holder, holder hung clear of the table, leads coiled off the floor, stub can emptied, plate returned to the rack.

Day 3 — Graded practical and written assessment

Objective for the day: Students demonstrate the unit outcomes under assessment conditions.

Warm-up (5 minutes)

Two practice beads to re-establish machine settings and arc length from Day 2. Not graded.

Graded booth practical (30 minutes)

Each student runs three flat-position stringer beads on 1/4" mild steel, scored against the visual inspection rubric: bead width consistency, absence of undercut, complete slag removal, no visible porosity at the toes. Two of three beads must meet criteria to pass.

Written assessment (15 minutes)

Ten-item written assessment covering equipment identification, electrode classification, polarity and PPE. See the unit assessment and its answer key.

Differentiation

Students who reach criteria early progress to a padding exercise — overlapping beads to fill a 2-inch square — which builds travel-speed control without introducing a new position. Students needing more time receive a pre-set machine so their practice is spent on arc length and travel rather than setup, with setup re-assessed separately later in the semester.

Materials for this unit

  • 1/4" mild steel plate, 4" x 6" coupons, 8 per student
  • 1/8" E7018 electrodes, stored in the rod oven at 250°F
  • 1/8" E6011 electrodes for the AC demonstration machine
  • Welding helmets, shade 10 minimum, one per booth
  • Leather gauntlet gloves, flame-resistant jackets, safety glasses worn under the helmet
  • Chipping hammers and wire brushes, one per booth
